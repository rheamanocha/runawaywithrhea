Overview

Your story: why the Ring Road, who you went with, how you got around, where you slept, rental car notes, and the one-line vibe.

Eight days around Iceland in September. Iceland has waterfalls in every direction, glaciers you can walk to, black sand beaches, and thermal lagoons to soak in. This guide covers the stops worth making, plus where to eat, what to bring home, and what to pack.

We started with Reykjavik and the Blue Lagoon, took a day on Snaefellsnes, then did the Golden Circle and drove the Ring Road counterclockwise from the south coast, through the East Fjords, and across the north. We finished through West Iceland and back to Reykjavik. If you would rather not drive the whole thing, you can stay in Reykjavik and take short trips on tours, particularly to the Golden Circle.

Tips if you go

  • Bring really good layers and warm accessories. I loved my gear from Halfdays. Make sure it’s really waterproof in case of rain, and expect strong wind.
  • You don’t really need proper snow boots, but a decent lightweight waterproof hiking boot helps. I wore my Timberlands.
  • Snaefellsnes can get extremely windy, so be careful opening your car door.
  • Decide whether to drive the Ring Road or stay in Reykjavik and take short trips. Tours are available.
  • Stop at grocery stores when you can if you’re driving the Ring Road. Food can be expensive, and most stops are heavier on meat and seafood.
  • Northern lights season is fall and winter, and puffins are seasonal. We were too late for puffins in September.
  • Get the gas station fries and put the spice on top.
